About Me

Hello! I am a graduate student in the Computational Science, Engineering, and Mathematics (CSEM) PhD program at the Oden Institute at UT Austin. I work with George Biros, where I am developing fast surrogates for solving chemical networks with error guarantees. I am also a member of the NSF-Simons AI Institute for Cosmic Origins (CosmicAI). My main research interests are numerical linear algebra, high-performance computing and scientific machine learning.

Prior to joining Oden, I received my undergraduate degree at the Colorado School of Mines, where I graduated summa cum laude with a B.S. in Computational and Applied Mathematics and a minor in Physics. I did undergraduate research with Steve Pankavich, where I worked on using active subspace methods to analyze sensitivities of plasmas arising in tokamaks and stellarators.

I have been lucky to complete two internships at Los Alamos National Laboratory. My first was in undergrad, with Tariq Aslam and Eduardo Lozano, through the Computational Physics Summer Workshop. I worked on efficient algorithms for solving equations of multiphase closure. Three years after, I returned to the lab as a CNLS summer student to work with Jamie Lilly and Mark Petersen on vertical turbulence modeling using machine learning.

Outside of work, I enjoy running, playing ultimate frisbee, and cheering for UT football.
